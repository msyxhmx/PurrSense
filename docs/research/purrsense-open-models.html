The best open-source models for PurrSense

Free, commercially usable open models cover detection, cat identification, breed and the on-phone explainer. Nothing open exists for pain or mood. Every good model or dataset built specifically for cat faces is non-commercial or has no licence, so labelled data and licences decide what we can ship, more than model accuracy does.

Builds on: Cat face analysis algorithmsFull report: reports/Open source models for PurrSense.md

Can we ship it with open models?

Ready to use
Detection · ID · breed · explainer

Apache-2.0 or MIT models exist. We still have to fine-tune them and convert them to run on the phone.

Blocked by licence
Facial landmarks

A ready-made phone model exists, but it's non-commercial because CatFLW is. We have to license the data or label our own photos.

Nothing exists
Pain · mood

No public grimace-scale models or data, and no credible cat mood classifier. We have to build both.

Key evidence

Why these picks

General open models nearly match cat specialists at telling cats apart

Top-1 accuracy identifying 518 cats (Kudryavtsev et al., arXiv 2603.02270). All three are Apache-2.0.

PurrSense only has to choose among 2 to 10 registered cats, which is easier than 518. Treat these numbers as a floor.

Gemma 4 E2B is the only explainer with official phone speed figures

Gemma 4 is now Apache-2.0; Gemma 3, 3n and PaliGemma 2 had more restrictive terms. It runs alongside Firebase AI Logic, not inside it. Its role stays the same: explain results, never score them. The untuned Qwen3-VL-2B read cat facial features correctly only about 26% of the time.

Licence traps

  • Ultralytics YOLO: its AGPL-3.0 licence covers models you train yourself too. A closed-source app needs a paid Enterprise licence.
  • CatFLW and anything trained on it is CC BY-NC 4.0, including the new phone-ready landmark model. The earlier plan to "train landmarks on CatFLW" only works commercially with a licence from the Tech4Animals Lab at the University of Haifa.
  • RF-DETR's smallest sizes (Atto, Femto, Pico) use Roboflow's own licence, which charges for commercial self-hosting.
  • Start D-FINE from its COCO-only weights. Its Objects365-pretrained weights may carry that dataset's terms.
  • DINOv3 allows commercial use but requires a visible "Built with DINOv3" notice.
  • Oxford-IIIT Pet is CC BY-SA (ShareAlike). Ask counsel whether that clause applies to trained weights.
Our work

What PurrSense has to provide itself

  1. Landmark data

    48-point cat-face labels, licensed from Haifa or annotated in-house. This determines the timeline.

  2. Pain data

    Owner photos scored by vets on the grimace scale, with no cat in both training and test data. Requesting Steagall's 1,188 scored images is the fastest start.

  3. Mood data

    Positive/negative labels that come from the situation the cat was in, not from someone judging its face.

  4. ID photos

    Consented photos from our own users to fine-tune the ArcFace head.

  5. Conversions

    LiteRT and Core ML exports. The transformer-based detector (D-FINE) and ViT backbones carry the most risk.

Suggested orderPrototype the whole pipeline now with the non-commercial landmark model and Steagall's published pain method, and measure accuracy on owner photos against vet scores. Then decide whether to license CatFLW or annotate our own, because that's where most of the cost is.

Gaps in the evidence

  • No phone speed figures for D-FINE, RF-DETR, DINOv2 or SigLIP2, and none for mid-range phones on any model.
  • Licences not checked against the model's own LICENSE file: RTMPose, YOLOX, Qwen3-VL-2B, and the Roboflow cat-face datasets.
  • Unknown whether Haifa or the Steagall lab offer commercial terms.
  • Catellect-VL-2B (a Qwen3-VL-2B fine-tune) beats frontier models at reading cat behaviour, but it was trained and tested on the same six cats and gives no pain score. Treat it as a future direction.
