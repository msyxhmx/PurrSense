# Licensing, not accuracy, decides PurrSense's model stack

PurrSense can ship most of its pipeline on permissively licensed open models. The best commercially safe pick for each job is: **D-FINE-N/S (Apache-2.0)** for the cat-face detector; **RTMPose or ViTPose (Apache-2.0) architectures** trained on landmarks PurrSense owns or licenses; **DINOv2-Small (Apache-2.0, 22M parameters)** with an ArcFace head for re-identification; **BioCLIP 2 (MIT)** as the teacher for breed and the "is this a cat?" gate; and **Gemma 4 E2B (Apache-2.0) on LiteRT-LM** as the on-device narrator. The open community offers **nothing usable for pain or mood**. No public Feline Grimace Scale (FGS) weights or image sets exist, and the only cat-emotion classifiers are hobby models at about 64% accuracy on unknown labels, so PurrSense has to build both itself. The hard truth is that **every high-quality cat-face-specific asset is non-commercial or unlicensed**. That covers the CatFLW landmark dataset and the first public weights trained on it, the PetFace ArcFace cat model, MegaDescriptor, SuperAnimal and MiewID. The technical path is therefore de-risked, but the legal path is not. PurrSense's real costs lie in four places: a 48-point landmark dataset (licensed from Haifa or annotated in-house), vet-scored FGS photos, context-labelled valence data, and consented re-ID photos from its own users. This revises one point from the earlier pipeline recommendation: "train the landmark detector on CatFLW" is only commercially viable with a licence from CatFLW's authors.

## The best pick per job, with runner-up and licence

The table condenses the recommendation. "Commercially safe" means the weights or code carry Apache-2.0, MIT or CC BY terms on their primary source. It does not mean the training data has been audited.

| Job | Best pick (ship) | Runner-up | Prototype-only (non-commercial / unlicensed) | Avoid for a closed-source app |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cat-face detection | D-FINE-N/S (Apache-2.0, 4–10M params), fine-tuned on CC BY 4.0 cat-face data | RF-DETR-Nano (Apache-2.0, 30.5M); a CNN detector as the low-risk fallback for conversion | hugocornellier `cat_face_localizer.tflite` (CC BY-NC) | Ultralytics YOLO (AGPL-3.0), YOLOv9 (GPL-3.0), MegaDetector MDv1000 (GPL/AGPL runtimes) |
| 48 facial landmarks | RTMPose-t/s (Apache-2.0, unverified) trained on PurrSense-owned or Haifa-licensed landmarks | ViTPose++-S (Apache-2.0) | hugocornellier `cat_face_landmarks_full.tflite` (CC BY-NC, NME 3.48) | SuperAnimal-Quadruped (academic only) |
| Re-ID embeddings | DINOv2-S/14 (Apache-2.0) + ArcFace on face crops | SigLIP2-B/16 vision tower (Apache-2.0) | PetFace `cat.pt` (research only), MegaDescriptor-L-384 (CC BY-NC) | MiewID (no licence), MobileCLIP (research-only weights) |

| Narrator VLM | Gemma 4 E2B (Apache-2.0) on LiteRT-LM | Qwen3-VL-2B / Qwen3.5-2B (Apache-2.0) | Catellect / Scalpel-VL (contradictory or unknown licence) | FastVLM, Moondream (until legal review) |

## Detection and landmarks are solved technically but blocked legally

**Detection.** The two strongest permissive detectors are D-FINE and RF-DETR. **D-FINE-N has 4M parameters and reaches 42.8 COCO AP; D-FINE-S has 10M and 48.5 AP.** Both are Apache-2.0 and export to ONNX ([Peterande/D-FINE](https://github.com/Peterande/D-FINE)). RF-DETR's Nano, Small, Medium and Large sizes are also Apache-2.0, with Nano at 48.4 AP. But Nano still has 30.5M parameters. RF-DETR's genuinely phone-sized variants (Atto, Femto, Pico) sit under Roboflow's Platform Model License, which requires an Enterprise add-on for commercial self-hosting ([roboflow/rf-detr](https://github.com/roboflow/rf-detr)). That makes D-FINE-N/S the better fit for a single-photo app. The usual default, Ultralytics YOLO, is a trap: Ultralytics states that **AGPL-3.0 covers even custom-trained or fine-tuned weights**, so a closed app needs a paid Enterprise licence ([Ultralytics License](https://www.ultralytics.com/license)). YOLOv9 is GPL-3.0 ([WongKinYiu/yolov9](https://github.com/WongKinYiu/yolov9)). PurrSense should also start D-FINE from COCO-only checkpoints, because its README warns that the Objects365-pretrained checkpoints "may be subject to the Objects365 dataset terms."

Neither DETR-family model documents a TFLite/LiteRT or Core ML export, and no phone latencies have been published (all figures are T4 TensorRT). Converting the transformer decoder via onnx2tf or coremltools is a real engineering risk. A CNN detector such as YOLOX-Nano or a MediaPipe/SSD-MobileNet model is the low-risk fallback; both are Apache-2.0 by reputation, but that was not verified against primary sources here. A one-photo app tolerates 50–100 ms of detection latency, which widens the options.

For training data, two Roboflow Universe single-class cat-face sets are listed as **CC BY 4.0**, which allows commercial use with attribution ([cat-face-xejom](https://universe.roboflow.com/cat-test/cat-face-xejom); [cats-faces-pqdnj](https://universe.roboflow.com/project1373/cats-faces-pqdnj)). This licence was read from search snippets, so the project pages need confirming. **Oxford-IIIT Pet** adds head boxes for 12 cat breeds under CC BY-SA 4.0 ([VGG Oxford Pets](https://www.robots.ox.ac.uk/~vgg/data/pets/)). Open-vocabulary models such as OWLv2 or Grounding DINO can auto-label more boxes offline.

**Landmarks.** This is the most consequential update since the earlier report. Mobile-ready CatFLW weights now exist. In September 2026, **hugocornellier/cat-face-landmarks** released a two-stage TFLite pipeline: an EfficientNetB2 face localizer (16 MB, IoU 0.81) and a MobileNetV3-Large 48-point landmark model (11 MB, 384 px). The landmark model reaches **NME 3.48 (3.31 with test-time augmentation) against 2.91 for the paper's ELD ensemble**, in 3.82 ms on GPU. Both are float16 exports built for the LiteRT GPU delegate ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/hugocornellier/cat-face-landmarks)). The models also ship inside the `cat_detection` Flutter package for Android and iOS. But both carry **CC BY-NC 4.0**, inherited from CatFLW, and the package page states it "can't be used in a commercial product." Commercial permission has to come from the Tech4Animals Lab at the University of Haifa ([pub.dev cat_detection](https://pub.dev/packages/cat_detection)). CatFLW itself is CC BY-NC 4.0 ([GitHub martvelge/CatFLW](https://github.com/martvelge/CatFLW)), so retraining on it does not escape the restriction.

The permissive route is to separate architecture from data. **RTMPose** reaches 0.722 AP on AP-10K at 256 px and was designed for mobile ([mmpose configs](https://github.com/open-mmlab/mmpose/tree/main/configs/animal_2d_keypoint/rtmpose)). **ViTPose++** is Apache-2.0, with ViTPose-S at 71.4 AP on AP-10K ([ViTPose](https://github.com/ViTAE-Transformer/ViTPose)), but its ViT backbone is heavier on phones. Neither has open cat-face data to learn from. AP-10K has only three facial keypoints ([AP-10K](https://github.com/AlexTheBad/AP-10K)), and its licence is contested: GitHub says CC BY 4.0, MMPose's documentation says non-commercial ([MMPose docs](https://mmpose.readthedocs.io/en/0.x/tasks/2d_animal_keypoint.html)). It is only marginally useful for pretraining. SuperAnimal-Quadruped is labelled "Modified MIT" but grants use "for academic, non-commercial purposes only" ([HF SuperAnimal](https://huggingface.co/mwmathis/DeepLabCutModelZoo-SuperAnimal-Quadruped)). The hugocornellier weights still have value: they are the right **prototype and benchmark** baseline, so PurrSense can validate the full pain pipeline on owner photos before paying for annotation.

## Re-ID and breed run on Apache and MIT foundation models

**Re-ID.** Every cat-specific re-ID model is off-limits. The PetFace ArcFace weights, including `cat.pt`, are restricted to "non-commercial research only" ([PetFace](https://dahlian00.github.io/PetFacePage)). **MegaDescriptor-L-384 is CC BY-NC 4.0** and has 229M parameters ([HF](https://huggingface.co/BVRA/MegaDescriptor-L-384)). **MiewID-msv3 declares no licence at all**; its repo shows only a copyright line, so default all-rights-reserved applies ([HF](https://huggingface.co/conservationxlabs/miewid-msv3); [GitHub](https://github.com/WildMeOrg/wbia-plugin-miew-id)). Apple's MobileCLIP is attractive on size (an 11.4M encoder at 1.5 ms on iPhone), but its weights are licensed "exclusively for Research Purposes," excluding "any commercial product or service" ([LICENSE_MODELS](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/apple/ml-mobileclip/main/LICENSE_MODELS)).

General-purpose backbones nearly match the specialists on cats. In a 518-cat held-out benchmark, **DINOv2-Small scored 85.5% top-1 (EER 0.042), SigLIP2-B 86.6% and SigLIP2-Giant 89.0%** ([arXiv 2603.02270](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02270)). DINOv2-Small is Apache-2.0 with 22.1M parameters ([HF](https://huggingface.co/facebook/dinov2-small)). SigLIP2-B is Apache-2.0 but about four times larger in the vision tower ([HF](https://huggingface.co/google/siglip2-base-patch16-224)). On size-to-accuracy and licence cleanliness, DINOv2-S wins, and an ArcFace fine-tune on aligned cat-face crops should close much of the gap to the earlier PetFace figure of 98% verification AUC. That closure is an inference, not a measured result. DINOv3-S is a credible alternative. Its custom licence permits commercial use, but it requires a prominent "Built with DINOv3" notice, prohibits military and ITAR uses, and adds an indemnity clause ([Meta DINOv3 License](https://ai.meta.com/resources/models-and-libraries/dinov3-license/)). It also has no published cat benchmark. The cleanest way to approach Giant-level accuracy on device is to distil SigLIP2-Giant (Apache-2.0) into the ViT-S or a MobileNet student on PurrSense's own data. PurrSense's closed-set task, matching among a household's 2–10 registered cats, is easier than the 518-cat benchmark, so these numbers are a floor rather than a ceiling.

**Breed and the cat gate.** **BioCLIP 2** is the most useful permissive animal foundation model: an MIT-licensed OpenCLIP ViT-L/14 trained on TreeOfLife-200M, actively maintained in 2026 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/imageomics/bioclip-2)). In the Bio-VQA benchmark it beats the best general VLM at taxonomy, **0.874 vs 0.731 at class level and 0.544 vs 0.269 at scientific name** ([arXiv 2611.05559](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05559)). That makes it the right zero-shot "is there a cat?" gate and the right teacher for breed. At ViT-L size it is too heavy to ship as-is, so the practical design is to fine-tune it on Oxford-IIIT Pet's 12 cat breeds, then distil a small breed head onto the shared DINOv2-S embedding. The runner-up, **dima806/cat_breed_image_detection**, is Apache-2.0 and ready today ([HF](https://huggingface.co/dima806/cat_breed_image_detection)). But it was trained on scraped images of unknown provenance, so the Apache tag on the weights says nothing about the data. SpeciesNet's repo is Apache-2.0 and covers Felidae, but it targets camera traps, has no mobile export, and its Kaggle weight licence was not confirmed ([google/cameratrapai](https://github.com/google/cameratrapai)). Oxford-IIIT Pet's ShareAlike clause clearly binds redistributed datasets; whether it reaches trained weights is legally unsettled and needs counsel.

## Pain and mood have no open models worth adopting

**Pain.** No public FGS model or dataset exists, and a Hugging Face search for cat pain returns nothing. The leading system, Steagall et al. 2023, uses 37 landmarks, 35 geometric descriptors and XGBoost to reach **95.5% binary accuracy and AUROC 0.97**. Its data are "not publicly available due to the undergoing development of the mobile phone application", though "available from the corresponding author on reasonable request." No code was released ([PMC10703818](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10703818/)). The Haifa group's pain work, on more naturalistic data, reports a more sober **66–77%** and also released no weights ([Sci Rep 2024](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s41598-024-78406-2); [Frontiers 2024](https://frontiersin.org/journals/veterinary-science/articles/10.3389/fvets.2024.1442634/full)). PurrSense therefore builds this stage itself, using the published recipe: geometric descriptors from the landmark stage, one gradient-boosted classifier per action unit, and a summed 0–10 total. The recipe is published, but the labels are not. Owner-photo accuracy will land below Steagall's curated clinical figure.

**Mood.** The open options are hobby-grade. The best-documented, **semihdervis/cat-emotion-classifier, is a ViT-base at 63.5% validation accuracy on an undescribed custom dataset** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/semihdervis/cat-emotion-classifier)). Its licence is fine; its science is not. No scientifically labelled cat-emotion image dataset with a commercial licence was found. The earlier recommendation stands: a binary or three-way valence head on the shared landmarks, modelled on Haifa's social-valence work, trained on labels that come from context rather than from someone judging a face.

**Could a fine-tuned VLM extract the cues instead?** The most interesting new evidence is **Catellect-VL-2B**, a fine-tune of Qwen3-VL-2B. It reaches **83.6% average accuracy on observable cat-behaviour fields (ears 92%, face 90%) against 53.7% for its untuned base and about 80–81% for Gemini 3 Pro, GPT-5.4 and Claude Opus** ([arXiv 2608.22070](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22070)). The caveats are severe:

- **Narrow data:** six household cats, with the same cats in training and test.
- **Machine labels:** most labels come from an LLM teacher rather than a vet.
- **No pain output:** it maps fields to emotion through a rule base and never scores FGS.
- **Slow:** 32 s per image on a Rockchip board, with no phone result.

A possibly related release, Scalpel-VL-1.6B, has contradictory MIT/Apache licence metadata and no released dataset ([HF](https://huggingface.co/freeai-org/Scalpel-VL-1.6B)). What Catellect shows is that a vet-designed, structured fine-tune can make a 2B open VLM useful for reading ear and eye positions. It is a research direction for a later version, not a substitute for dedicated FGS classifiers.

## Gemma 4 E2B is the narrator that fits both platforms

Gemma 4 E2B is the clear narrator pick. It is **Apache-2.0**, a notable break from the restrictive Gemma Terms of Use that governed Gemma 3, 3n and PaliGemma 2. It is also the only open VLM with first-party latency figures on both an Android and an iOS flagship. Through Google's LiteRT-LM runtime, the 2.58 GB file reaches **52 tok/s decode and 0.3 s time-to-first-token on a Galaxy S26 Ultra GPU, and 56 tok/s with 0.3 s TTFT on an iPhone 17 Pro GPU**. LiteRT-LM also converts custom fine-tuned safetensors to `.litertlm`, which leaves a route to ship a cat-tuned narrator later ([LiteRT-LM Gemma 4](https://developers.google.com/edge/litert-lm/models/gemma-4)). It comes from the same vendor as PurrSense's current Gemini/Firebase stack, but it would sit beside Firebase AI Logic rather than inside it. The larger E4B is too slow on CPU, at 5–6.5 s TTFT.

The runner-up is the Qwen family. **Qwen3.5-2B (Apache-2.0) scores 64.2 MMMU and 81–83 MMBench** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-2B)). Qwen3-VL-2B runs at about 41–43 tok/s on Snapdragon 8 Elite through Qualcomm AI Hub ([HF](https://huggingface.co/qualcomm/Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct)), on Android through llama.cpp, and on iOS through MLX conversions. It is also Catellect's base model. Its Apache-2.0 licence was inferred rather than read from its own card. These scores cannot be compared directly with Gemma's, because Google reports MMMU-Pro and Qwen reports standard MMMU. A community review also reports generation loops on Qwen3.5-2B.

**MiniCPM-V 4.6** (1.3B, Apache-2.0, official iOS and Android demos) and **SmolVLM2** (256M–2.2B, Apache-2.0) are the fallbacks for older phones ([HF MiniCPM-V 4.6](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/MiniCPM-V-4.6); [HF SmolVLM2](https://huggingface.co/blog/smolvlm2)). **Apple's FastVLM** ships its weights under a separate Apple model licence whose terms were not verified ([apple/ml-fastvlm](https://github.com/apple/ml-fastvlm)). **Moondream** uses BSL 1.1 or a custom licence ([HF](https://huggingface.co/moondream/moondream3-preview)). Both need legal review before shipping.

Three unknowns matter. All latency figures come from 2026 flagships, so mid-range performance is unmeasured. Google's tables do not break out image-encoding time. And a 2.6 GB model will probably need on-demand download rather than bundling (an inference). The narrator role itself is not in doubt: untuned Qwen3-VL-2B scored about 26% on cat facial fields in the Catellect benchmark, which confirms that small VLMs should phrase results, not produce them.

## What PurrSense must build, license or annotate

**Annotate or license, in priority order:**

1. **A 48-point cat-face landmark set.** This is the critical path. Either negotiate a commercial licence for CatFLW and its derivative weights with Tech4Animals (whether Haifa offers one, and on what terms, is unknown), or annotate a PurrSense-owned set following the CatFLW scheme. The CatFLW papers document the scheme, but the training-code repo's licence could not be verified.
2. **Vet-scored FGS photos taken by owners**, with a train/test split by cat. Requesting Steagall's 1,188 scored images under a research or data agreement is the fastest start. Commercial terms are unknown.
3. **Context-labelled valence data** for the mood head.
4. **Consented re-ID photos from PurrSense's own users**, to fine-tune the ArcFace head.

**Build:**

- the detector fine-tune;
- the landmark model on RTMPose;
- the FGS feature extractor and per-action-unit classifiers;
- the valence head;
- the ArcFace re-ID head and per-household enrolment/threshold logic;
- the BioCLIP-to-student breed distillation;
- the LiteRT and Core ML conversions.

The conversions are where the DETR detector and ViT backbones carry the most risk.

**Legal housekeeping:**

- bundle NOTICE and licence texts, because an on-device model is redistribution;
- attribute CC BY data;
- get counsel on Oxford-IIIT Pet's ShareAlike clause and on any weights touched by CC BY-NC data;
- record the provenance behind every Apache- or MIT-tagged community model before adopting it.

## Conclusion

The open-source community has commoditised the generic layers of PurrSense's pipeline: detection, embedding backbones and narration. It has not touched the feline-specific layers. Where cat-face work exists, academic labs have released it under non-commercial terms, or (in Steagall's case) withheld it to build their own app. PurrSense's moat, and its main budget line, is therefore labelled data rather than model architecture. The cheapest sequencing follows from that. Prototype the whole pipeline now with the non-commercial hugocornellier landmarks and the published Steagall recipe, and measure owner-photo accuracy against vet scores. Only then commit to buying a CatFLW licence or annotating in-house, since that test will show how much landmark precision the pain score actually needs.

A second implication is strategic. Gemma 4 moving to Apache-2.0, combined with LiteRT-LM running on both platforms, lets PurrSense keep its Google alignment while leaving Gemini Nano's device restrictions behind. A vet-designed structured fine-tune like Catellect's could later turn that same narrator into a second, explainable reader of facial cues, provided PurrSense's own data, rather than six cats, is behind it.
